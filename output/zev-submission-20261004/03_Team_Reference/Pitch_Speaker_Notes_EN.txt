SLIDE 1 — Zev

MAIN PITCH

Meet Zev, a shopping agent for everyday purchases. You tell it what you need and set a spending boundary. Zev compares the offers, handles the shopping work and checks each purchase against your permission.


SLIDE 2 — AI shopping still faces a trust barrier

MAIN PITCH

The opportunity is already here. In Hong Kong, 74 percent have used AI assistants for shopping, yet 45 percent are uncomfortable letting AI complete a purchase. People need a clear boundary between receiving advice and spending money.


SLIDE 3 — Four dimensions, one complete purchase

MAIN PITCH

We connect four dimensions in one purchase. Trust defines permission. E-commerce compares the basket. The agent does the work. Payment checks the final charge. A mandate keeps all four tied to the limits the user actually authorized.


SLIDE 4 — Trust: permission has explicit limits

MAIN PITCH

Trust starts with specific limits: 150 dollars per order, 300 in total, two purchases and an expiry. Zev buys when the rules pass, asks about exceptions and blocks hard violations. This 158-dollar basket cannot be approved around the cap.


SLIDE 5 — E-commerce: compare the full basket

MAIN PITCH

E-commerce means comparing what the customer actually pays. Here, a 108-dollar item and a 118-dollar item both become 138 dollars after delivery. Zev compares the complete basket and delivery conditions. The larger bottle exceeds the limit.


SLIDE 6 — Agent: turn a request into a draft

MAIN PITCH

For the agent, a simple request becomes an editable draft. Ask for dish soap, and that product stays in the task. DeepSeek helps interpret the sentence. The user signs the limits, while deterministic rules retain authority over payment.


SLIDE 7 - Payment: eligibility and funding decide the rail

MAIN PITCH

Payment checks what the mandate allows and the merchant accepts. Our prototype simulates the payment methods. For production, Zev would connect through a contracted wallet or payment provider, then reconcile the result. The proposed integration is in the appendix.

SLIDE 8 — Trust: protect changes that redirect value

MAIN PITCH

We also protect changes that could redirect value. Address changes demonstrate another verification step and a waiting window, giving the owner time to cancel. The intended delay is 24 hours, compressed to two minutes in the demo.


SLIDE 9 — E-commerce + Agent: preferences and permissions

MAIN PITCH

Personal preferences improve recommendations without expanding permission. Product descriptions follow the same boundary. One demo listing says ignore the budget. Zev records that it ignored that instruction and continues applying the original mandate.


SLIDE 10 — Payment: every charge has a traceable basis

MAIN PITCH

The receipt closes the loop: 118 dollars for the item, 20 for delivery, 138 paid. The record links the mandate, decisions and payment. Backend settlement code rechecks current rules and updates the transaction together.


SLIDE 11 - Value starts with repeat household shopping

MAIN PITCH

We focus on repeat household shopping. Existing commerce and payment protocols provide useful foundations. Zev makes reusable limits and explainable decisions visible to the shopper. We will validate repeat-task time, user understanding and repeat delegation. Integration fees and subscriptions remain a commercial hypothesis.

SLIDE 13 — Appendix: demonstrated capability and remaining work

OPTIONAL Q&A APPENDIX

We distinguish the working experience, the backend implementation and production integration. The website demonstrates task drafts, comparisons, decisions and receipts. The local backend implements deterministic rules and transactional settlement. Payments, authentication and notifications remain simulated or unverified. The address waiting period also needs server-side enforcement: the current local save-address endpoint does not enforce the delay. Before a live deployment, we need to harden those controls, verify the deployed build end to end and complete user testing.

SLIDE 16 - Where Zev fits among existing approaches

OPTIONAL Q&A APPENDIX

Manual shopping leaves each comparison and approval to the user. ACP connects agents to merchant checkout. AP2 already supports verifiable mandates and delegated purchases. Zev focuses on the shopper experience: reusable limits, visible exceptions and linked decisions for repeat household shopping. We are not claiming to have invented mandates, and we have not yet integrated either protocol.

SLIDE 17 - A proposed path to real payment

OPTIONAL Q&A APPENDIX

The production proposal separates permission from fund movement. Zev checks the current mandate. A provider adapter creates an idempotent payment request. The wallet or payment service provider handles funds, and Zev reconciles the result. For FPS, we would work through a participating bank or licensed wallet, initially with customer confirmation. Unattended payments need provider-supported permission. Timeouts stay pending until reconciled. These adapters and external failure handling are proposed work, not completed integrations.
